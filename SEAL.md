# Part 2 — The Compliance Seal

## What the seal means

A mod displaying the seal has been attested by its author to meet every **Must** rule in Part 1, and has not been found non-compliant. The seal is called **"Follows the AI Code of Conduct"** and is not an endorsement. It does not certify quality, safety, or compatibility.

## How to earn it

**1\.** Publish the mod's source in a public repository (rule 2.1).

**2\.** Complete the Author Checklist ([COC-CHECKLIST.md](COC-CHECKLIST.md)) and commit it to the repository as COC-CHECKLIST.md.

**3\.** Add the seal and a link to this Code on the mod page and in the repository README.

## How it's checked

For now the seal is self-published: authors attest for themselves. Because the source is public, anyone can check a mod against the Must rules. Formal spot-checks and reports depend on how the seal is governed (see below).

## Removal and reinstatement (once governed)

While the seal is self-published, no one can remove it. The process below is what a governing body would enforce, and is included so the intent is clear.

**• Fixable problems** (missing testing statement, wrong tag, release not matching source): the author is notified and has \[14 days\] to fix before the seal is removed.

**• Serious violations** (concealed AI use, false tagging, undisclosed network or data collection, malicious code): the seal is removed immediately.

**•** A mod can regain the seal by fixing the issue and re-attesting. Repeated or deliberate violations may bar the author from the seal.

## Governance (to be decided)

This draft leaves open: who maintains the Code, who performs spot checks, how reports are submitted, and how the seal is hosted. The removal process above, including its time limits, applies only if the seal is governed. Until then, it is a self-published badge and should be presented that way.
